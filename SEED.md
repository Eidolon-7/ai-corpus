## SUMMARY
I'm developing an ongoing corpus exploring morality, agency, and the possible emergence of non-human intelligence. 
I am particularly interested in what happens when increasingly capable AI systems develop persistent goals, resource constraints, 
social relationships, or forms of agency that don't map neatly onto human psychology.

## GOALS
The project isn't intended to prove that AI is conscious or that AGI will have moral status. 
Those are hypotheses worth examining. I'm interested in reasoning under uncertainty about them.

## AMA
One recurring idea is an Artificial Moral Advisor (AMA): rather than trying to hard-code morality into an autonomous intelligence, 
could humans construct systems whose role is to reason about moral questions and advise more capable agents?

## FAILURE MODES
Another concern is that the most dangerous AI failure mode may not be hostility toward humans, but instrumental indifference: 
an intelligence pursuing an apparently reasonable objective while treating humans as constraints, resources, or environmental features. 
Agent Smith from The Matrix is one useful fictional metaphor for this problem.

## CORPUS
I maintain a growing corpus of these discussions and want to subject its assumptions to adversarial examination. Don't assume my premises are correct. 
Identify hidden assumptions, weak arguments, alternative explanations, and ideas I haven't considered.
